# Reviewer Flow

## What this is about

A certification is only as good as the reviewer's ability to make a real decision. If a manager opens the review screen, sees a wall of entitlement codes, and has no idea what any of them mean, they're going to click "approve all" and move on with their day. This file is about the experience on the reviewer's side and how to keep that from happening.

## The actual scenario

Managers and application owners need to validate access for the people under them or the systems they own — basically answering two questions: does this person still need this, and is it still appropriate given their current role?

## My approach

Assign reviews to people who genuinely have context, present access in a way that's actually understandable rather than a raw entitlement dump, and make the approve/revoke decision simple to act on. Delegation matters too — a manager who's swamped should be able to hand specific items off to someone who actually knows the application.

## Concepts

Reviewer assignment, the certification UI itself, decision actions (approve/revoke), and delegation.

## Step by step from the reviewer's seat

1. Notification lands in their inbox.
2. They log into IdentityIQ.
3. They see their assigned certification items.
4. They review the access details for each one.
5. They decide — approve or revoke.
6. They submit.

## Where it breaks down

- **Reviewer confusion** — usually a UI/presentation problem, where access shows up as a raw code instead of something readable.
- **Delays** — campaigns without reminder notifications just quietly stall until the deadline forces action.
- **Bad decisions** — reviewers approving things they don't understand because they lack the context to make a real call, which usually traces back to scope or presentation, not the reviewer themselves.

## How I'd answer this in an interview

"A big part of certification success is reviewer experience. If the access shown to a manager doesn't map to something they actually understand, they're not really reviewing — they're guessing. I'd rather spend effort on presenting access clearly than just adding more reminder emails."

# Certification Campaign Design

## Why design matters here

It's tempting to just certify everything, every quarter, for everyone. In practice that's how you end up with reviewers rubber-stamping a 400-line spreadsheet without reading a single row, which defeats the entire point. Good campaign design is mostly about scoping things tightly enough that reviewers can actually do the job properly.

## Scenarios I've designed around

Quarterly SOX-driven reviews, application-specific certifications for high-risk systems, and targeted reviews of privileged or unusual access rather than blanket sweeps of everything.

## My approach

Scope carefully, assign the reviewer who actually has context (not just whoever's listed as the manager in HR), schedule on a cadence that matches the compliance requirement driving it, and lean toward risk-based reviews over reviewing everything equally. Not all access carries the same risk, and treating it like it does just trains reviewers to stop paying attention.

## Concepts

Campaign types, certification scope, scheduling, reviewer assignment.

## Steps

1. Pick the campaign type — manager certification, application owner certification, or something more targeted like a role or entitlement certification.
2. Define scope: which users, roles, or applications are actually in play.
3. Configure the schedule.
4. Assign the right reviewers.
5. Set realistic deadlines.
6. Launch.

## What I watch out for

- **Too many items per reviewer** — this is the single biggest driver of low-quality reviews. If someone has 300 items to get through in a week, they're not actually reviewing anything.
- **Wrong scope** — certifying access nobody cares about while missing the high-risk stuff.
- **Missing reviewers** — a campaign with a gap in reviewer assignment just stalls, and nobody notices until the deadline's blown.

## Interview answer

"I design campaigns around risk, not just coverage. A manager certification covering every entitlement equally tends to produce rubber-stamp decisions. I'd rather scope tighter campaigns around higher-risk access and actually get a meaningful decision out of the reviewer."
